## Who counts as unemployed?

Not everyone without a job is unemployed. Economists place each adult in one of
three groups:

- **Employed:** people who have a job
- **Unemployed:** people who do not have a job, are available to work, and have recently looked for one
- **Not in the labor force:** people who are neither employed nor unemployed, such as many full-time students and retirees

The **labor force** is everyone who is employed or unemployed. The exact age
limits and survey rules differ between countries.

## Measuring unemployment

The **unemployment rate** is the share of the labor force that is unemployed:

**Unemployment rate = (unemployed ÷ labor force) × 100**

The **labor force participation rate** is the share of the adult population
that is in the labor force:

**Participation rate = (labor force ÷ adult population) × 100**

### Example

Imagine a small country with 100 adults. Of these, 57 are employed, 3 are
unemployed, and 40 are not in the labor force.

1. Labor force = 57 + 3 = 60
2. Unemployment rate = (3 ÷ 60) × 100 = **5%**
3. Participation rate = (60 ÷ 100) × 100 = **60%**

Notice that the unemployment rate is divided by the labor force, not by all adults.

## Three types of unemployment

- **Frictional:** it takes time for workers and jobs to find each other. Example: a graduate searching for a first job.
- **Structural:** workers' skills or locations do not match the available jobs, or wages stay too high for everyone who wants a job to find one. Example: a factory worker whose skills are no longer needed after machines take over the task.
- **Cyclical:** total spending in the economy falls, so firms need fewer workers. Example: a hotel worker laid off during a recession.

To tell them apart, ask **why** the person is out of work: are they still
searching, is there a lasting mismatch, or is the whole economy weak?

## The natural rate of unemployment

Even a healthy economy has some unemployment. The **natural rate of
unemployment** is made up of frictional and structural unemployment:

**Natural rate = frictional + structural**

Cyclical unemployment is the gap between the actual rate and the natural rate.
It is above zero in a recession, and it can be below zero when the economy is
unusually strong.

## What the unemployment rate leaves out

- **Discouraged workers** have stopped looking because they believe no jobs are available. They are counted as not in the labor force.
- **Underemployed workers** include part-time workers who want full-time work. They are counted as employed.

Because of this, the unemployment rate can fall even when no one finds a job:
some people may simply have stopped looking.
