## What is inflation?

**Inflation** is a sustained rise in the overall price level. One price going up
is not inflation; inflation means prices are rising on average.

- **Deflation:** the overall price level falls
- **Disinflation:** prices still rise, but more slowly than before

## Measuring inflation

The **consumer price index (CPI)** tracks the cost of a fixed basket of goods
and services that a typical household buys.

**CPI = (cost of basket this year ÷ cost of basket in base year) × 100**

**Inflation rate = ((new CPI − old CPI) ÷ old CPI) × 100**

### Example

A basket costs 200 in the base year, 210 in year 1, and 231 in year 2.

1. CPI in year 1 = (210 ÷ 200) × 100 = 105
2. CPI in year 2 = (231 ÷ 200) × 100 = 115.5
3. Inflation in year 2 = ((115.5 − 105) ÷ 105) × 100 = **10%**

### Limits of the CPI

- **Substitution:** people switch to cheaper goods, but the basket stays fixed.
- **New goods:** new products may take time to enter the basket.
- **Quality changes:** a higher price may partly reflect a better product.

## What causes inflation?

- **Demand-pull:** total spending grows faster than the economy can produce.
- **Cost-push:** production costs rise, such as a jump in oil prices.
- **Money growth:** over the long run, sustained high inflation is usually linked to rapid growth in the money supply.
- **Expectations:** if people expect inflation, they may ask for higher wages and prices, which helps keep inflation going.

## Nominal and real values

**Nominal** values are measured in current money. **Real** values are adjusted
for inflation. For interest rates, a useful approximation is:

**Real interest rate ≈ nominal interest rate − inflation rate**

If a bank pays 6% and inflation is 4%, the real interest rate is about 2%.
The savings can buy only about 2% more than before.

## The costs of inflation

Even a steady rise in prices can be costly:

- **Menu costs:** firms spend time and money changing prices.
- **Shoe-leather costs:** people make extra effort to hold less cash.
- **Confusing price signals:** it is harder to see which goods have become relatively more expensive.
- **Taxes:** some tax systems tax gains that only reflect inflation.

**Unexpected** inflation also moves wealth between people. With a fixed interest
rate, higher-than-expected inflation helps borrowers and hurts lenders, because
loans are repaid in money that buys less.

If wages rise as quickly as prices, purchasing power does not fall. Inflation
hurts most when incomes do not keep up.
