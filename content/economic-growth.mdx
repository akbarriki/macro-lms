## What is economic growth?

**Economic growth** is an increase in an economy's real output over time. To
compare living standards, economists often use **real GDP per person**:

**Real GDP per person = real GDP ÷ population**

Its growth rate is roughly real GDP growth minus population growth. If real GDP
grows 5% and population grows 1%, real GDP per person grows about 4%.

## Small differences add up

Growth builds on itself each year. The **rule of 70** estimates how long it
takes for something to double:

**Years to double ≈ 70 ÷ annual growth rate in percent**

### Example

- At 2% growth, income doubles in about 70 ÷ 2 = **35 years**
- At 5% growth, income doubles in about 70 ÷ 5 = **14 years**

## Productivity is the key

**Productivity** is how much output is produced per hour of work. In the long
run, rising productivity is the main source of higher living standards. It
depends on:

- **Physical capital:** tools, machines, buildings, and roads
- **Human capital:** workers' education, skills, and health
- **Natural resources:** land, water, minerals, and energy
- **Technology:** knowledge about better ways to produce things

Natural resources help, but they are not required. Some economies with few
resources have become rich through skills, trade, and technology.

## Why capital alone is not enough

Capital has **diminishing returns**: when other inputs stay the same, each extra
machine adds less output than the one before. A worker's first computer helps a
lot; a third one helps very little.

This has two important results:

- Saving and investing more can raise growth for a time, but not forever. Lasting growth in output per person depends on better technology and ideas.
- Poorer countries with little capital may grow faster than richer ones. This **catch-up effect** is possible, but not automatic.

## Policies that can support growth

- Encouraging saving and investment
- Improving education and health
- Protecting property rights and the rule of law
- Supporting research and new ideas
- Allowing trade and competition

Many of these have a cost today. For example, saving more means consuming less
now in return for more later.

## Growth is not everything

Growth can bring better health, education, and opportunities. But it may also
harm the environment, and its benefits may be shared unequally.
