## What is monetary policy?

**Monetary policy** is how a central bank influences interest rates, money, and
credit. In Indonesia the central bank is Bank Indonesia; in the United States it
is the Federal Reserve.

Many central banks aim for low and stable inflation. Some also have goals such
as high employment, a stable currency, or a stable financial system.

## What is money?

Money has three functions:

- **Medium of exchange:** people accept it as payment
- **Unit of account:** prices are measured in it
- **Store of value:** it can be saved and used later

Banks create money when they make loans. In a simple model where banks lend all
extra reserves and the public holds no cash:

**Money multiplier = 1 ÷ reserve ratio**

With a reserve ratio of 10%, the multiplier is 1 ÷ 0.10 = 10. This is a maximum.
In practice, lending depends more on interest rates, regulation, and demand for
loans.

## The main tools

- **Policy interest rate:** the rate the central bank sets to guide other short-term rates
- **Open market operations:** buying or selling securities. Buying adds reserves to banks; selling removes them.
- **Reserve requirements:** the share of deposits banks must hold as reserves
- **Lending and deposit facilities:** rates for banks that borrow from, or deposit at, the central bank
- **Unconventional tools:** for example, large purchases of longer-term assets, called **quantitative easing**

Countries use different mixes of these tools.

## Two directions

- **Expansionary (easing):** lower interest rates to raise aggregate demand
- **Contractionary (tightening):** higher interest rates to reduce inflation

## How policy reaches the economy

When the central bank **raises** interest rates, it works through several channels:

- **Borrowing:** loans cost more, so firms invest less and households buy fewer homes and cars.
- **Asset prices:** prices of shares and property tend to fall, so people feel less wealthy.
- **Exchange rate:** higher returns may attract foreign funds. The currency strengthens, so net exports tend to fall.
- **Expectations:** if people trust the central bank, they expect lower inflation.

Aggregate demand falls, and inflation slows. A rate cut works in the opposite
direction.

### Example

Inflation is 7% and the target is 3%. The central bank raises its policy rate.
Over time spending slows and inflation falls toward 3%, but unemployment may
rise for a while.

## Limits

- **Time lags:** the full effect can take a year or more.
- **Lower bound:** interest rates cannot easily go far below zero.
- **Supply shocks:** fighting cost-push inflation with higher rates also lowers output.
- **Long run:** monetary policy mainly affects prices. It cannot raise potential output permanently.

Many economists support **central bank independence**, because it can protect
decisions from short-term political pressure and keep inflation expectations low.
